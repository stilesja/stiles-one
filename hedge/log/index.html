Hedge: Build Log

Engineering events documented as they happen. The evergreen architecture picture lives in the case study; this is what actually happened along the way.

  • August 8, 2026 The math was honest. The words were not.

    The scoring never celebrated being right. The copy quietly did, at every surface a new player meets first. Nobody decided that: celebrating correctness is the default gravity of games, and there is no test for an app that subtly disagrees with itself.

  • August 2, 2026 The second half of the promise

    Hedge 1.0 shipped the mirror; charts are homework. The new badge roster is the coaching half: thirty-six badges where nothing pays out for luck, the grind itself is the training, and every future badge retro-grants the morning it ships.

  • August 2, 2026 I deleted the app to fix the app

    The phone review app was a thin HTTP client in an app costume. One reframe deleted the whole native ceremony: a web app served off the Mac mini, one URL for every device, in production the same afternoon. This week the old app retires.

  • August 2, 2026 The badge that wouldn't forgive

    One predicate, three designs: a lifetime gate that couldn't forgive early miscalibration, a fixed window that made tier three merely slower, and the version where the window is the count. Plus a simulator pass that became a design studio.

  • July 24, 2026 I deployed a ruler

    Three reasonable fixes and a black bar that survived them all. A temporary diagnostics card made the iPhone report its own geometry: 47 points of screen no web pixel was allowed to paint, all traced to one status-bar meta tag.

  • July 24, 2026 The $112 night

    One overnight agent run put every subagent on the flagship model and burned $112. The next phase ran deliberately tiered for about $18, caught every bug that mattered, and left an allocation rule: tier by blast radius, not difficulty.
