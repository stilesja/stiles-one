Privacy policy

Last updated: October 25, 2026

The short version

Hedge collects no data. Everything stays on your devices and, if you use iCloud sync, in your own private iCloud. During a Hedge Party, players' names, photos, and bets travel only between the devices in the room, over your own Wi-Fi. We never see any of it.

The long version

What we collect

Nothing. There are no user accounts, logins, or profiles. Your answers, scores, calibration data, and streaks are stored locally on your device and, when iCloud sync is on, in the private iCloud storage of your own Apple account. Neither copy is transmitted to us and we cannot see either one. We run no servers.

Network access

Hedge does not transmit your data to any server. The app makes no network requests for analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, advertising, or any third-party service. New question batches may be delivered over the air over your normal device connection, but no information about you is sent in those requests beyond what the operating system itself transmits when checking for updates.

Notifications

If you opt in to daily reminders in Settings, Hedge schedules local notifications on your device. These do not require an account and do not send any data off the device — iOS handles the scheduling locally.

Hedge Party

Hedge Party is a multiplayer mode for people in the same room. One device, an Apple TV, a Mac, or an iPad, is the stage, and everyone else plays from their own phone. The devices find each other and connect directly over your local network using Bonjour. The first time you open Party, iOS asks for permission to use the local network; Hedge uses it only to find and connect to parties.

To join, you pick a player name and, if you like, a photo. You can take the photo with the camera or choose one from your library; Hedge asks for access only when you tap to add one, and nothing else in your library is read. Your name, photo, and bets are sent only to the devices in that party, never to a server. The stage shows names and photos to the room and keeps the photos on that device so faces appear again at the next party. Phones keep the party packs and the photos of people they have played with, on the device, for the same reason.

Party games do not touch your daily answers, streak, or calibration, and nothing from a party is sent to us. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on that device.

Apple TV

The Hedge Party app for Apple TV is the stage only. It stores the party name you choose and, as described above, the names and photos of players who join. It has no accounts, sends nothing to any server, and includes no analytics, advertising, or tracking.

Cloud sync

Hedge can keep your play history in step across your devices using iCloud. Sync is on by default when you are signed into iCloud, and you can turn it off any time in Settings inside the app.

Sync uses Apple's CloudKit private database and iCloud's key-value store: your data is stored under your own Apple ID, alongside your other iCloud data. It is never shared with us or with anyone else, and we have no ability to read it. Apple hosts it; we do not.

Because the data lives in your iCloud account, reinstalling the app or setting up a new device signed into the same Apple ID restores your history automatically. To remove the cloud copy, delete Hedge's data from iCloud in iOS Settings (your Apple ID, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage).

Third-party SDKs

Hedge is built with Expo and React Native, plus a small set of open-source libraries for fonts, icons, and on-device audio playback. None of these libraries collect or transmit user data in the way Hedge uses them. Hedge includes no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting SDKs.

Children

Hedge is rated 4+ and contains no advertising or in-app purchases. The only content players create is a party name and an optional photo, which are shown only to the people in the same room, on their own devices. Hedge is safe for children but does not specifically target them. We collect no data from any user, regardless of age.

Changes to this policy

When iCloud sync was added (July 2026), this section promised the change would be announced in the app's release notes before shipping, and it was. When Hedge Party was added (October 2026), this policy was updated before the release, and Party still collects nothing. The same holds going forward: if Hedge ever starts collecting data, this policy will be updated and the change announced in the app's release notes before the new version ships.
